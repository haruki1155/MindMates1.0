# Why the Quick Assessment asks five questions

The Quick Assessment is a brief well-being reflection. Each answer contributes one indicator; together they help a student or employee notice patterns and decide whether a fuller assessment or PACC support may help. The result is not a diagnosis.

## Emotional calm

**Question:** In the past two weeks, how often have you felt calm and relaxed?

This asks whether there has been room to rest and recover. Its response scale draws on the WHO-5 well-being area. A lower calm response can suggest that rest or support deserves attention.

## Stress load

**Question:** In the past two weeks, how often have you felt overwhelmed by everything you had to do?

This asks whether tasks feel manageable. It draws on the perceived-stress area measured by the Perceived Stress Scale. Noticing overload early can help someone plan, prioritize, or ask for help.

## Social connection

**Question:** In the past two weeks, how connected have you felt to the people around you at school or work?

This asks about everyday social connectedness. Supportive relationships can help people handle difficult days; feeling disconnected may be a reason to reach out.

## Motivation and interest

**Question:** In the past two weeks, how often have you had little interest or pleasure in doing things you usually enjoy?

This draws on the interest area of the PHQ-2. Changes in enjoyment can be useful to notice, especially when they persist or affect daily life.

## Daily coping

**Question:** In the past two weeks, how often has stress from school or work made it hard to handle your daily life?

This asks about functional impact: whether stress is getting in the way of ordinary activities. It helps distinguish feeling stressed from having difficulty coping day to day.

These five custom items **have not been formally validated as a combined screening tool**. Their wording, interpretation, and use should be reviewed by PACC professionals before clinical claims or decisions are made from them.
